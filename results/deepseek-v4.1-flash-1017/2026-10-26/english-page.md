Together with this gigantic transfer, a total of nearly 14,000 persons, including Japanese and Manchurians, had passed into the employment of Manchoukuo, either directly or indirectly. Among them may be mentioned 1,396 police officers and men from the Japanese Embassy, 3,723 police officers and men from the Kwantung Bureau, 150 judicial officers, 1,500 postal officers and employees of the Kwantung Bureau, 340 tax office officers and employees, and 6,884 officers and employees of the Local Administration Bureau of the South Manchuria Railway Company. The last figure included an army of 2,663 teachers who were under the employment of the South Manchuria Railway Company and who had passed into the employment of the School Associations and Federation of School Associations.

Of the above mass transfer of personnel, the largest number naturally came from the South Manchuria Railway Company. On November 30, 1937, therefore, solemn ceremonies were held at Dairen and all other centers where the S. M. R. Local Administration Offices are located to bid an official farewell to the departing employees of the Company. And following this, the Local Administration Bureau of the South Manchuria Railway Company, with its thirty-year history of brilliant pioneering and achievement, had been dissolved.

The negotiations over various institutions which the South Manchuria Railway Company has maintained for experimental purposes, which were to be retained by the Company for the time being, had progressed smoothly after the above great transfer and a perfect understanding was reached between the South Manchuria Railway Company and the Manchoukuo Government by March, 1938. As a result, the second great transfer was made on April 1, 1938. It involved the following institutions: (1) the Kungchuling Agricultural Experimental Station; (2) the Hsiungyaocheng Agricultural Experimental Station; (3) the Yamuyingtzu Agricultural Experimental Station; (4) the Liaoyang Cotton Experimental Station; (5) the Fenghuangcheng Tobacco Experimental Station; (6) the Taonan Agricultural Experimental Station; (7) the Linhsi Sheep Breeding Farm; (8) the Kungchuling Sheep Breeding Farm; (9) the Veterinary Institute; (10) the Geological Research Institute; (11) the Tumenling Forestry Experimental Station; and (12) the Hsingcheng Horticultural Experimental Station. With the above, the Health Research Institute was transferred to the Kwantung Bureau and the Kwantung Army. These institutions which served as the basis of various industrial activities in the past were evaluated at 3,637,000 yen but were to be “loaned” to the authorities mentioned without compensation for the time being. With them, an additional number of 445 S. M. R. employees passed into the employment of Manchoukuo or the Kwantung Bureau.

Thus passed into history Japan’s extraterritorial rights in Manchoukuo and her administrative rights over the S. M. R. Zone. It is to the credit of all parties concerned, their spirit of cooperation and careful preparations, that such an epochal transition of first magnitude was consummated with an amazing smoothness, even without a slightest hitch or difficulty. And the satisfactory result of the procedure has strongly underwritten Japan’s trust placed upon Manchoukuo, as well as a phenomenal progress made in all phases of Manchoukuo’s statehood during its short span of six years.
